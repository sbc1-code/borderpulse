# San Ysidro-Tijuana workflow discovery cohort

Status: internal research packet, no outreach or offer sent

## Purpose

Test one commercial question without building more software:

> Do passenger-transport or parking operators around San Ysidro have a repeated
> customer or dispatch decision that public wait-time widgets do not solve?

This is not a sales list and it is not evidence that any company needs
BorderPulse. It is a small, research-backed cohort for five discovery
conversations once Sebastian separately authorizes outreach.

## Why this segment

The selected businesses operate recurring passenger journeys across the
San Ysidro-Tijuana corridor. Their own websites describe schedules, routing,
cross-border coordination, or customer communication around fluctuating wait
conditions. That makes the segment a better test than a generic local business
that simply wants a live-widget display.

Healthcare providers and FastLane sellers are deliberately out of the first
cohort. The discovery does not need patient information, appointment details,
or travel documents. Tijuana's [official FastLane page](https://sedeti.tijuana.gob.mx/fastlane)
says commercialization, promotion, or misuse of a pass is prohibited.
BorderPulse must never issue, sell, market, or operate a FastLane/medical-pass
service.

| [MexLuxRides](https://www.mexluxrides.com/tijuana) | Private cross-border chauffeur service states it uses real-time flight and border tracking, route/lane selection, buffers, and bilingual concierge communication. | How are border changes communicated before and during a booked trip, and could a public-facing buffer/status page reduce routine customer questions? | Its existing concierge and tracking process may make a new public surface redundant. BorderPulse must not claim guaranteed crossing time. |
| [XicoTransport](https://www.xicotransport.com/en) | Bilingual operator serving companies, maquiladoras, airport, border-crossing, and tourism journeys with multi-passenger vans. | Do corporate/group travelers need a shared, plain-language corridor briefing before a crossing, and who owns it today? | The firm may not run recurring dispatch itself, or its need could be solved by current WhatsApp/driver practice at no added value. |

This mix intentionally includes a direct-link control (Border Station Parking),
a fixed-schedule operator (SDTJ Passport), two higher-service dispatch models,
and a B2B/group operator. A common job must appear across at least three
accounts before considering an implementation pilot.

## Interview protocol

Do not pitch BorderPulse, quote price, request analytics, or ask for customer,
medical, booking, payment, passenger, driver, or travel-document data.

Open with the current workflow:

1. When a border condition changes, what decision changes for your customers,
   dispatch, or drivers?
2. Who checks what source today, how often, and what do they then do?
3. Which customer questions recur before a scheduled trip or return journey?
4. Does current wait information ever change a route, pickup time, buffer,
   staff response, or the recommendation the customer receives?
5. What is missing from the free tools and direct official links you use now?
6. If a narrow, source-attributed status or briefing page removed that manual
   work, who would own it and how would you know it helped?

Only if a specific downstream job appears, test commitment:

7. Would you review a one-corridor, manual pilot with a stated source,
   fallback, support boundary, and no prediction or pass promise?
8. What result would justify paying for that maintained implementation rather
   than continuing with your present tools?

## Qualification and stop rules

Advance only if all of these are true:

- five conversations are completed in this one segment;
- three accounts independently identify the same decision after a border change;
- at least one says free alternatives or existing internal practice are not
  enough and accepts a written pilot scope;
- the job can be delivered from public, attributed data without handling
  sensitive passenger or medical information;
- no part of the offer depends on a FastLane/medical-pass entitlement,
  prediction, or a guaranteed crossing time.

Stop commercial work if the repeated answer is simply “we link to CBP,” “our
driver handles it,” or “a free widget is enough.” That is useful negative
evidence. Keep BorderPulse as a free product and do not create accounts,
billing, providers, or a Vercel production cutover.

## If the gate passes

Build one manual, buyer-owned implementation only after a written commitment.
The scope should specify the named corridor, official source, visible
freshness, static fallback, no-guarantee language, buyer-owned next-step copy,
support contact, duration, and the agreed observation of value. It must not
need Stripe subscriptions, a public API, user accounts, health data, or a
claim that BorderPulse controls border access.
