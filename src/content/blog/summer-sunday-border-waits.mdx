## Archived July 3, 2026 analysis

This article originally described a 30-day Standard passenger sample. Its exact snapshot start and end dates were not retained in the article source. The figures below are preserved as the original publication record, not a newly verified sample or advice for this weekend.

[Check the latest lane reports](/) or [open the published historical planning views](/best-time/). Current reports and historical charts have separate source clocks and coverage.

## Figures reported in the original article

- **San Ysidro:** the article quoted a Sunday median of 150 minutes at 8 AM, 180 at 1 PM, and 30–45 around 3–5 AM.
- **Otay Mesa:** it quoted 45–75 minutes through 9 AM, 155 at 6 PM, 190 at 7 PM, 200 at 9 PM and 170 at 11 PM.
- **Tecate:** it quoted 255 minutes at 2 PM, 310 at 3 PM and 275 at 4 PM.
- **Paso del Norte:** it quoted 60–85 minutes through Sunday afternoon/evening and 15–20 around 4–6 AM.
- **Nogales DeConcini:** it quoted 20–50 minutes during Sunday daytime, 90 at 7 PM and 120 at 9 PM.

These numbers do not establish current conditions, a guaranteed minimum wait, or the best departure time.

## Plan with the lane you can actually use

Compare the same eligible lane at nearby crossings, check its CBP report clock and snapshot retrieval age, and consider the extra driving. Do not assume Ready Lane or SENTRI will always be faster.
