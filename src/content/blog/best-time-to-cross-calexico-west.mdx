Calexico West is the Imperial Valley's downtown crossing, the one most Mexicali daily commuters use to get into California. The median northbound wait over the last 30 days sits at **70 minutes**, and the lightest dependable window all week is **early weekend morning: Sunday 5 AM medians 10 minutes, with Saturday 5 AM at 28 and Sunday 4, 6, 7 and 8 AM all at 30.** The pattern runs opposite to commuter lore: the port is at its calmest before 9 AM, waits build from mid-morning, the heavy wall holds through the afternoon, and evenings ease back down.

The chart below is the last 30 days of Calexico West northbound wait times from U.S. Customs and Border Protection, broken out by day of week and hour of day. Green = quick (under 30m), amber = typical, red = heavy. Hover any cell for the median number.

## What the data actually says

Three patterns jump out:

1. **Sunday before dawn is the clearest "best time" at this port.** **Sunday 5 AM medians 10 minutes**, the lightest cell in the grid, and it holds under 40 from 4 AM through 9 AM. Saturday follows the same shape a little heavier, bottoming at **28 minutes at 5 AM**. Pooled across all seven days, the best hour on the clock is 4 AM at 45.
2. **Wednesday is the problem day, not the afternoon commute.** Wednesday's day median is **105 minutes** against 60 for Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Friday and Saturday. It runs heavy from before dawn, holding **120 from 6 AM straight through 11 AM** and peaking at **140 at noon**. Tuesday builds a narrower version of the same wall, climbing from 60 at 8 AM to **138 at 1 PM**. If your plan was "wait out the commute, cross mid-morning," Wednesday and Tuesday say the opposite.
3. **Sunday afternoon return traffic is mild here.** Sunday holds a flat 60 from 10 AM through midnight, with the afternoon dipping to 45 at 2 PM. That is unusual for a border port and it makes Calexico West a reasonable Sunday option. Monday evening is the one to watch instead, drifting up to the **mid 70s to 90 after 8 PM**.

If you want to plan around a specific hour, the [Calexico West live page](/crossing/calexico-west) shows the current wait alongside the same 30 day pattern.

## Calexico West vs. Calexico East

Imperial Valley has two CBP crossings, and the choice is not always obvious.

- **Calexico West (downtown)** is the historic crossing and the closer one to downtown Mexicali. Heavier traffic, older infrastructure, but shorter total trip if your destination is in El Centro or central Imperial Valley.
- **[Calexico East](/crossing/calexico-east)** is newer, designed for cars and commercial traffic, and tends to run shorter waits on weekdays. It is the better choice if you are flexible on the U.S. side route and want predictable times.

Run both live cards in tabs and pick the lower number when you actually leave. The Border Pulse [main dashboard](/) shows them side by side.

## Standard, Ready Lane, and SENTRI

Calexico West has all three lanes, and the lane choice matters more here than at most ports because the standard-lane variance is so wide.

- **Ready Lane** at Calexico West runs a meaningful percentage faster than the standard lane during weekday midday peaks (typically 40 to 60 percent). The lane is well-staffed.
- **SENTRI** is the safety lane for the weekday midday wall. When the standard lane is running 120 to 140 minute medians on a Tuesday or Wednesday afternoon, SENTRI savings vs. standard can approach two hours.

Eligibility and document rules for both programs are CBP's, not ours. Read the official pages before you bank on a lane.

<OfficialSource agency="U.S. Customs and Border Protection" url="https://www.cbp.gov/travel/clearing-customs/ready-lanes">
  Ready Lane is for travelers with an RFID-enabled travel document. CBP maintains the official list of acceptable documents, lane hours per port, and rules for passengers in the vehicle.
</OfficialSource>

<OfficialSource agency="U.S. Customs and Border Protection" url="https://www.cbp.gov/travel/trusted-traveler-programs/sentri">

## Holidays, advisories, and the agricultural season

Hour-of-week medians do not capture one-off events. The biggest ones at Calexico:

- **Semana Santa (Holy Week)** and **Christmas / Paisano season (Dec 1 to Jan 10)** push waits well past the medians shown above.
- **Imperial Valley harvest season** changes the commercial-lane mix and the staffing pattern. Live waits during peak harvest can spike outside the typical pattern.
- **U.S. federal holidays** can change CBP staffing. Hours posted on the [official port page](https://bwt.cbp.gov/) take precedence.

The DOS travel advisory for Mexico is updated periodically and applies at the state level. Check it before any first trip.

<OfficialSource agency="U.S. Department of State" url="https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/mexico.html">
  The State Department publishes a travel advisory for Mexico with state-level guidance and a list of restricted areas. Read the full advisory and any active U.S. embassy security alerts before crossing, especially for first time trips or stays outside the immediate border zone.
</OfficialSource>

## How we compute these numbers

Border Pulse pulls the official CBP wait time for Calexico West and stores a snapshot. The chart above is the **median** wait per (day of week, hour of day) cell across the last 30 days of those snapshots, built from about 380 individual samples. Because a 7 by 24 grid is finer than the refresh cadence can fill, each cell is estimated from a window centred on that hour, widened to at most two hours either side and only as far as it needs to go. Cells that still fall short of five observations are shown as "not enough data" instead of a number. We never pool across days, because the day of week is the pattern. The window is rolling, so these figures move as new snapshots arrive and older ones age out. The numbers quoted on this page were recomputed on July 27, 2026; the live chart above always reflects the current window. Median is more honest than average for this data because a single 240 minute outlier can drag a true 70 minute typical hour past 90.

The raw data and methodology live in [our public repo](https://github.com/sbc1-code/borderpulse). Snapshots come from [CBP's public BWT feed](https://bwt.cbp.gov/), which is the canonical source.

## What to do with this

The single most useful thing you can do with this chart at Calexico West: **if you can cross before 9 AM, do it, and if you can make it a weekend morning, even better.** The savings vs. the midday wall are large and consistent. The early window is also when the standard lane and Ready Lane are nearly tied, so you do not need to pay the cost of a Trusted Traveler enrollment to capture the win.
