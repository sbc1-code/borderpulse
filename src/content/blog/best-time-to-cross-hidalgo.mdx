The Hidalgo international bridge is the McAllen / Reynosa workhorse, and the data tells a different story than the California crossings most travelers compare it to. The median northbound wait over the last 30 days sits at **55 minutes**, lower than any major SoCal port. The lightest hour of the day, pooled across the whole week, is **8 AM with a 20 minute median**, and the early block runs light across every day: from about 2 AM to 9 AM, hourly medians hold between 15 and 40 minutes. The pattern is less a spike than a wall: waits climb from mid-morning and hold at 50 to 65 minutes from about 10 AM into the night, seven days a week. Real relief only shows up after midnight.

The chart below is the last 30 days of Hidalgo northbound wait times from U.S. Customs and Border Protection, broken out by day of week and hour of day. Green = quick (under 30m), amber = typical, red = heavy. Hover any cell for the median number.

## What the data actually says

Three patterns jump out:

1. **Early mornings run at about a quarter of the afternoon price.** The standout is **Wednesday, where 6 through 9 AM all median 15 minutes**, against an around-the-clock median of 55. Friday 6 and 7 AM sit at **18**, and Sunday 6 and 7 AM at **20**. The pattern holds every day of the week: before 9 AM you pay a fraction of what the afternoon costs, and at Hidalgo that gap is wider than at any California port.
2. **The peak is a plateau, not a spike.** From about 10 AM to 10 PM, hourly medians hold at 50 to 65 minutes nearly every day, and the evening barely releases. The worst cells are **Monday 4 PM at 75 and 3 PM at 73**, with Monday 5 PM at 70 across fifteen observations, the deepest support in the grid. Weekends give you only a small break: Sunday afternoons ease to 50 after 3 PM. By day, Friday and Wednesday run heaviest (60) and Sunday and Tuesday lightest (45).
3. **The Hidalgo overall median (55 min) is well under San Ysidro (120), Otay Mesa (90), or Calexico West (70).** Volume and lane structure mean Hidalgo absorbs traffic better. The trade-off: when there is a backup at Hidalgo, it tends to be harder to predict because it usually comes from a one-off (advisory, lane closure, weather) rather than a steady commute curve.

## Hidalgo, Pharr, and Anzalduas: which McAllen-area bridge

The McAllen / Reynosa region has three CBP crossings, each with a different role.

- **[Hidalgo](/crossing/hidalgo-pharr-hidalgo)** is the central, highest-volume passenger and pedestrian crossing. The data here.
- **[Pharr](/crossing/hidalgo-pharr-pharr)** handles the bulk of the commercial / truck traffic, which is why it is consistently slower for passenger vehicles even though the bridge is newer.
- **[Anzalduas](/crossing/hidalgo-pharr-anzalduas-international-bridge)** is the western alternative; designed for cars, often the fastest for passenger trips when Hidalgo backs up. Worth comparing live before any trip.

For a more general comparison across the RGV, also check the live waits at [Brownsville Veterans International](/crossing/brownsville-veterans-international) and the [Progreso bridges](/crossing/progreso-progreso-international-bridge), which serve different downstream destinations.

## Standard, Ready Lane, and SENTRI

Hidalgo has standard, Ready Lane, and SENTRI lanes. The economics are different from the high-pressure CA crossings:

- **Standard lane** is fast enough early on Wednesday and Friday mornings (15 to 18 minutes) that the Ready Lane savings shrink to almost nothing. The savings show up against the afternoon plateau.
- **Ready Lane** is the safety net for the afternoon plateau, and Monday 3 to 5 PM in particular. Worth using if you have an RFID-enabled document; not necessary at 7 AM Wednesday.
- **SENTRI** is overkill for Hidalgo's typical pattern unless you cross daily during the worst hours.

Eligibility and document rules for both programs are CBP's, not ours. Read the official pages before you bank on a lane.

## Holidays, advisories, and the RGV-specific events

Hour-of-week medians do not capture one-off events. The biggest ones at Hidalgo:

- **Spring Break** brings significant northbound and southbound surges across all RGV crossings. Medians shown above will not hold during peak weeks.
- **Christmas / Paisano season (Dec 1 to Jan 10)** pushes waits well past the medians, especially southbound, since Hidalgo is a major Paisano corridor.
- **U.S. federal holidays** can change CBP staffing. Hours posted on the [official port page](https://bwt.cbp.gov/) take precedence.
- **Weather and bridge closures** are unique RGV risks during hurricane season and after heavy rains. Always check the per-port advisory before you leave.

The DOS travel advisory for Mexico is updated periodically and applies at the state level. Tamaulipas in particular has carried elevated advisories, so read the current text before any first trip.

## How we compute these numbers

Border Pulse pulls the official CBP wait time for Hidalgo and stores a snapshot. The chart above is the **median** wait per (day of week, hour of day) cell across the last 30 days of those snapshots, built from about 380 individual samples. Because a 7 by 24 grid is finer than the refresh cadence can fill, each cell is estimated from a window centred on that hour, widened to at most two hours either side and only as far as it needs to go. Cells that still fall short of five observations are shown as "not enough data" instead of a number. We never pool across days, because the day of week is the pattern. The window is rolling, so these figures move as new snapshots arrive and older ones age out. The numbers quoted on this page were recomputed on July 27, 2026; the live chart above always reflects the current window. Median is more honest than average for this data because a single 240 minute outlier can drag a true 60 minute typical hour up to 90.

The raw data and methodology live in [our public repo](https://github.com/sbc1-code/borderpulse). Snapshots come from [CBP's public BWT feed](https://bwt.cbp.gov/), which is the canonical source.

## What to do with this

The single most useful thing you can do with this chart at Hidalgo: **trust the early morning window, but pick your day.** Wednesday 6 to 9 AM medians 15 minutes and Friday 6 to 7 AM medians 18, which is as close to a no-wait crossing as this border gets. Thursday morning is not the same deal at 50, and Saturday morning sits at 43. Save the lane savings (Ready, SENTRI) for when you actually need them, which is Monday afternoon.
