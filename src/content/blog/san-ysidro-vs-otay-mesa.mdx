San Ysidro and Otay Mesa are two options for crossing from Tijuana to San Diego. Which works better for your trip depends on the latest reported wait, the lane you can use, and the drive to and from each crossing.

## Three checks before choosing

1. **Compare the same lane.** The [current comparison](/compare/san-ysidro-vs-otay-mesa/) shows reported standard-passenger waits. If you can use SENTRI or Ready Lane, check that lane on both crossing pages.
2. **Check the report time and operating status.** A stale or missing report is a reason to verify conditions. A closed lane or an unavailable wait is not a zero-minute queue.
3. **Include the extra drive.** Compare the wait difference with the additional travel to the crossing and onward to your destination. There is no fixed weekday or hour when one crossing is always the better choice.

## What about Tecate?

If Tecate fits your route, check its [current crossing report](/crossing/tecate/) too. Verify its operating hours and account for the extra drive before choosing it over San Ysidro or Otay Mesa.

## Lane choice changes the math

Standard passenger, Ready Lane, and SENTRI waits can differ. Check your eligibility before using a dedicated lane.

<OfficialSource agency="U.S. Customs and Border Protection" url="https://www.cbp.gov/travel/clearing-customs/ready-lanes">
  Ready Lane is open to travelers with an RFID-enabled travel document (U.S. Passport Card, Enhanced Driver's License from a participating state, certain Trusted Traveler cards, and others). CBP publishes the official acceptable document list and per-port lane availability. Always verify your document is on the current list before committing to the lane.
</OfficialSource>

<OfficialSource agency="U.S. Customs and Border Protection" url="https://www.cbp.gov/travel/trusted-traveler-programs/sentri">
  SENTRI is a Trusted Traveler Program. Membership requires an application, fee, background check, and in-person interview at an enrollment center. SENTRI lanes typically save the most time during peak hours. Membership rules apply to every occupant of the vehicle.
</OfficialSource>

For SENTRI or Ready Lane, open each crossing's current report and compare that specific lane. The rolling charts here do not provide a lane-by-lane comparison.

## How to read these charts

Border Pulse stores snapshots of official CBP wait reports. These charts use the most recently generated 30-day rolling aggregate, with **median** waits grouped by local day of the week and hour. Some hourly estimates pool nearby hours when observations are sparse. The displayed patterns can change whenever the aggregates are refreshed.

Use the charts as historical context. They do not reproduce the original April analysis or predict the queue you will encounter. A cell without a value means data is unavailable, not that the wait is zero. Check the latest reports at [bwt.cbp.gov](https://bwt.cbp.gov/) or on our [San Ysidro](/crossing/san-ysidro/) and [Otay Mesa](/crossing/otay-mesa/) pages before making a route decision.
