People from El Paso, Las Cruces and southern New Mexico cross into Ciudad Juárez for dental work as a day trip. The dentistry is the clinic's job. This guide covers the part around it: which bridge to use, what to bring, how to check a dentist before you book, and how to time the trip back.

## The short version

- **Pick the bridge by where you start and how you travel.** Walking from downtown El Paso: Paso del Norte. Driving: Bridge of the Americas or Ysleta-Zaragoza. Coming from New Mexico: look at Santa Teresa.
- **Bring a document CBP accepts for re-entry by land.** CBP lists them under the [Western Hemisphere Travel Initiative](https://www.cbp.gov/travel/us-citizens/western-hemisphere-travel-initiative).
- **Check the dentist's license before you book.** Mexico's education ministry runs a public [license lookup](https://www.cedulaprofesional.sep.gob.mx/).
- **Plan the return, not just the appointment.** The wait to come back is the part that varies.

## Which bridge to use

| Starting from | Crossing to check first | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Downtown El Paso, on foot | [Paso del Norte](/crossing/el-paso-paso-del-norte-pdn/) | It connects downtown El Paso to downtown Juárez, and CBP reports a pedestrian lane there. |
| Central El Paso, driving | [Bridge of the Americas](/crossing/el-paso-bridge-of-the-americas-bota/) | Known locally as Puente Libre. It handles passenger vehicles, including larger ones. |
| East El Paso, driving | [Ysleta-Zaragoza](/crossing/el-paso-ysleta/) | It is the eastern bridge, so it can save a drive across town. |
| Las Cruces or southern New Mexico | [Santa Teresa](/crossing/santa-teresa-santa-teresa-port-of-entry/) | It sits west of El Paso in New Mexico. Compare it with the El Paso bridges before you leave. |

Ask the clinic which bridge is closest to them and whether they offer a pickup. Some Juárez clinics do, and it changes which bridge makes sense.

If you are choosing between the two central bridges, the hour-by-hour comparison is in [Paso del Norte vs Bridge of the Americas](/blog/paso-del-norte-vs-bridge-of-the-americas/).

## What to bring

- **Your travel document.** U.S. citizens returning by land need a document CBP accepts, such as a U.S. passport book or passport card. The full list is on [CBP's page](https://www.cbp.gov/travel/us-citizens/western-hemisphere-travel-initiative).
- **Your dental records.** Recent X-rays and a list of the medications you take help the dentist plan before you sit down.
- **A way to pay that the clinic confirmed.** Ask ahead whether they take cards, dollars or pesos.
- **The clinic's address and phone number on paper.** Phone service can drop near the bridges.

## How to check a dentist before you book

Border Pulse does not rate clinics. These are checks you can do yourself.

1. **Look up the license.** Every dentist practicing in Mexico has a professional license number (cédula profesional). Ask for it and search it in the [public registry](https://www.cedulaprofesional.sep.gob.mx/). A specialist, such as an implant or oral rehabilitation dentist, has a separate specialty license. Ask for that one too.
2. **Ask for a written treatment plan and an itemized quote.** It should say what is included: X-rays, the implant, the abutment, the crown, follow-up visits.
3. **Ask what happens if something goes wrong after you are home.** Who do you call, is there a warranty, and does a fix mean another trip?
4. **Ask how many visits the work takes.** Implants and full restorations usually take more than one trip, so the crossing becomes part of the cost.
5. **Know where complaints go.** Mexico's medical arbitration commission, [CONAMED](https://www.gob.mx/conamed), handles disputes between patients and health providers.

## Timing the trip back

The appointment has a fixed time. The return wait does not. The chart shows the typical northbound wait at Paso del Norte by day and hour, from the last 30 days of CBP reports.

<BestTimeChart slug="el-paso-paso-del-norte-pdn" title="Paso del Norte: median northbound wait, last 30 days" />

- **Book around the quiet hours** the chart shows, when you have a choice.
- **Check the current wait before you leave the clinic**, not when you reach the line. Each crossing page shows the time CBP last reported.
- **Have a second bridge in mind.** If one is backed up, another may be shorter.

The chart is history, not a forecast. Holidays, weather and lane closures change it.

## Bringing medication back

If the dentist prescribes something, the rules for bringing it into the U.S. are in [Bringing prescription medication back from Mexico](/blog/bringing-prescription-medication-back-from-mexico/). Declare what you carry.
