Reviewed against CDC sources on October 29, 2026.

## Start with the previous six months

Check **every country the dog visited in the six months before U.S. entry**, using the [CDC navigator](https://www.cdc.gov/importation/dogs/index.html). A Mexico vaccination alone does not put a dog on the high-risk pathway.

## Only rabies-free or low-risk countries

CDC requires a Dog Import Form receipt. The dog must appear healthy, be at least six months old, and have a microchip readable by a universal scanner. CDC accepts any brand readable by a universal scanner; ask your veterinarian or chip company to confirm readability.

The receipt can cover multiple entries for six months while its conditions remain satisfied: no high-risk travel and the correct departure country. A different departure country requires a new receipt. Check the receipt’s expiration.

[Read the low-risk requirements](https://www.cdc.gov/importation/dogs/rabies-free-low-risk-countries.html) and [complete the form](https://www.cdc.gov/importation/dogs/form.html).

## Any high-risk country in that period

Use CDC’s separate high-risk requirements. Vaccination origin and required endorsed documents matter on that pathway; do not apply the low-risk checklist. Foreign-vaccinated dogs with high-risk travel cannot enter at a U.S. land border under CDC’s high-risk pathway. Follow the applicable CDC entry route.

[CDC FAQs](https://www.cdc.gov/importation/dogs/faqs.html) explain these distinctions. USDA and destination rules can also apply. This guide promises neither admission nor inspection timing.
