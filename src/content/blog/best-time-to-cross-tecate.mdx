Tecate has a reputation as the quiet alternative to the San Diego crossings. The current data says: only in the morning. The median northbound wait over the last 30 days sits at **120 minutes**, the same as San Ysidro, and the lightest hour all week is **7 AM with a 60 minute median.** The real pattern is a steep daily ramp: mornings run near an hour, afternoons roughly triple that, and the worst cell on the board is **Monday 8 PM at 260 minutes**, with Sunday evening close behind at 235.

The chart below is the last 30 days of Tecate northbound wait times from U.S. Customs and Border Protection, broken out by day of week and hour of day. Green = quick (under 30m), amber = typical, red = heavy. Hover any cell for the median number.

## What the data actually says

Three patterns jump out:

1. **The only reliably light window is early morning, and Friday owns it.** 7 AM has the lowest pooled median of any hour of the week at 60 minutes. The lightest cells on the board are **Friday 10 and 11 AM, both at 45**, with Friday 6 through 9 AM at a flat 60. Sunday and Tuesday match that 60 at 5 to 7 AM. By 10 AM on Sunday the median is already 180, so the window closes fast.
2. **The worst stretch is Monday evening, not Sunday afternoon.** **Monday 8 PM medians about 250 minutes, with 7 PM near 210 and 4 to 5 PM at 200**, the heaviest run at this port. Sunday evening is the runner-up, with 5 PM and 7 PM both at 235 and 6 PM at 225. Sunday afternoon itself is flatter than it used to look, holding a steady 180 from 10 AM through 3 PM. Monday and Sunday tie for the heaviest day at a 180 median.
3. **Friday is the escape hatch, and overnight is still a data desert.** Friday's day median is **98** against 180 for Monday and Sunday, and it is the only day that stays under two hours from midnight to midnight. Below 5 AM we mostly stay quiet on purpose: most days produce too few observations between midnight and 4 AM to publish a number, so those cells read "not enough data" rather than a guess.

If you want to plan around a specific hour, the [Tecate live page](/crossing/tecate) shows the current wait alongside the same 30 day pattern.

## How Tecate compares to San Ysidro and Otay

The San Diego trio is one trip planner, not three independent ports. Quick rules of thumb from the data:

- **Tecate is no longer the automatic Sunday escape valve.** [San Ysidro's](/crossing/san-ysidro) Sunday afternoon cells hold a flat 170 minutes; Tecate's run 180 through the afternoon and climb to 225 to 235 in the evening. If you are coming back from Valle de Guadalupe or Ensenada on a Sunday, the detour east only pays before 8 AM, when Tecate sits at 60 against San Ysidro's 100.
- **For weekday morning commutes, [Otay Mesa](/crossing/otay-mesa) now wins on both counts.** Otay's overall 30 day median is 90 minutes to Tecate's 120, and its Friday morning cells run 20 to 30 against Tecate's 60. Tecate's 6 to 8 AM weekday cells still run a respectable 60 to 90, but the early start no longer favors Tecate the way it did, and Otay keeps the SENTRI acceleration and Ready Lane hours that Tecate does not advertise as aggressively.
- **For pure tourist convenience, San Ysidro wins.** Tecate's town side is small and the U.S. side is rural until Highway 94. Going through Tecate adds drive time on both sides versus crossing closer to TIJ airport.

## Standard, Ready Lane, and SENTRI

Tecate is a smaller port and lane availability changes more often than at the big ports.

- **Ready Lane** is open at Tecate but the lane is single-vehicle and operates only during specific posted hours. Always verify the lane is staffed before you commit.
- **SENTRI** is available, and against the Monday and Sunday evening peaks, where the standard lane runs 220 to 260 minutes, the SENTRI lane is the difference between an hour and four.

Eligibility and document rules for both programs are CBP's, not ours. Check the official pages before you plan around a lane.

## Holidays, advisories, and the small-port quirk

Hour-of-week medians do not capture one-off events. The biggest ones at Tecate:

- **Semana Santa (Holy Week)** and **Christmas / Paisano season (Dec 1 to Jan 10)** push waits well past the medians shown above, especially for the southbound direction (Tecate is a popular Paisano route into Baja).
- **U.S. federal holidays** can change CBP staffing. Hours posted on the [official port page](https://bwt.cbp.gov/) take precedence.
- **Single-bus events** matter more here than at bigger ports. A 50-passenger tour bus arriving at 11 PM can quintuple the wait for the next 30 minutes. The official [CBP feed](https://bwt.cbp.gov/) is updated as conditions change; the live card on Border Pulse shows the current snapshot.

The DOS travel advisory for Mexico is updated periodically and applies at the state level. Check it before any first trip.

<OfficialSource agency="U.S. Department of State" url="https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/mexico.html">
  The State Department publishes a travel advisory for Mexico with state-level guidance and a list of restricted areas. Read the full advisory and any active U.S. embassy security alerts before crossing, especially for first time trips or stays outside the immediate border zone.
</OfficialSource>

## How we compute these numbers

Border Pulse pulls the official CBP wait time for Tecate and stores a snapshot. The chart above is the **median** wait per (day of week, hour of day) cell across the last 30 days of those snapshots, built from about 310 individual samples. Because a 7 by 24 grid is finer than the refresh cadence can fill, each cell is estimated from a window centred on that hour, widened to at most two hours either side and only as far as it needs to go. Cells that still fall short of five observations are shown as "not enough data" instead of a number. We never pool across days, because the day of week is the pattern. The window is rolling, so these figures move as new snapshots arrive and older ones age out. The numbers quoted on this page were recomputed on July 27, 2026; the live chart above always reflects the current window. Median is more honest than average for this data because one 310 minute outlier next to five 90 minute reads drags the average to about 127 while the median stays at 90.

Tecate logged about 310 snapshots in this window versus San Ysidro's 390, and it is a thinner port: 16 to 20 of its 24 hourly cells clear the five-observation floor on a given day, against a full 24 at San Ysidro. Every number quoted above rests on at least five observations. The day-of-week patterns are the stable signal; the exact lightest-hour-by-day call may still shift as the rolling window updates.

The raw data and methodology live in [our public repo](https://github.com/sbc1-code/borderpulse). Snapshots come from [CBP's public BWT feed](https://bwt.cbp.gov/), which is the canonical source.

## What to do with this

The single most useful thing you can do with this chart at Tecate: **check the live wait first, then check the typical wait for that same hour.** When the gap is large in either direction, that is your decision signal. A live 60-minute wait at an hour where the typical is 150 minutes is a window worth taking; a live 2-hour wait at an hour where the typical is 60 says wait or reroute.
