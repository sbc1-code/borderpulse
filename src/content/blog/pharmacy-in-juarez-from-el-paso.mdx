People from El Paso, Las Cruces and southern New Mexico walk into Ciudad Juárez to buy medicine. Two sets of rules apply: Mexico's rules decide what the pharmacy can sell you, and U.S. rules decide what you can bring back. This guide covers the Mexican side and the crossing. The U.S. side is in [Bringing prescription medication back from Mexico](/blog/bringing-prescription-medication-back-from-mexico/).

Nothing here is medical advice. Talk to your own doctor or pharmacist about any medicine you plan to buy.

## The short version

- **Antibiotics need a prescription in Mexico.** That has been the rule since August 2010, and the pharmacy keeps or stamps it.
- **Some medicines need a special prescription** that only certain Mexican doctors can write, and not every pharmacy sells them. Do not count on buying them as a visitor.
- **Many pharmacies have a doctor's office next door.** A consultation there can produce a Mexican prescription. It does not change what the U.S. lets you bring back.
- **Declare what you carry** when you cross back, and keep it in its original packaging.

## What Mexico requires at the counter

Mexico's General Health Law sorts medicines into groups. What matters at the counter:

| Kind of medicine | What the pharmacy needs |
|---|---|
| Over-the-counter medicine | No prescription. |
| Most prescription medicine | A prescription from a doctor licensed in Mexico. |
| Antibiotics (pills and injections) | A prescription. The pharmacy keeps it or stamps it. This rule took effect on August 25, 2010. |
| Controlled medicines (strong painkillers, some sedatives and stimulants) | A prescription the pharmacy keeps. For the most controlled group, a special prescription with a barcode that only authorized doctors can write. |

Two things follow from the table:

1. **A U.S. prescription is not a Mexican prescription.** The pharmacy follows Mexican rules. Ask before you assume a U.S. paper will work.
2. **Rules change.** For example, Mexico's health regulator, COFEPRIS, moved tramadol into the controlled category in July 2026. Check before a trip if your medicine is a painkiller, a sedative or a stimulant.

## Pharmacies with a doctor next door

After the 2010 antibiotic rule, many Mexican pharmacy chains added a small doctor's office (consultorio) next to the counter. COFEPRIS publishes a [guide for how these offices must operate](https://www.gob.mx/cms/uploads/attachment/file/915663/GU_A_DE_FARMACIAS_CON_CONSULTORIOS_ADYACENTES_2020_abril___1_.pdf).

- **The visit is a real medical consultation.** The doctor should examine you, ask about your history and the medicines you already take, and write a prescription only if it fits.
- **A Mexican prescription helps in Mexico, not at the U.S. booth.** What you can bring back still depends on U.S. rules, covered in the [prescription guide](/blog/bringing-prescription-medication-back-from-mexico/).
- **Ask the doctor for their professional license number** (cédula profesional) if you want to check it. Mexico's education ministry runs a public [license lookup](https://www.cedulaprofesional.sep.gob.mx/).

## Before you buy

- **Bring a list of what you take now**, with doses, so the pharmacist or doctor can check for interactions.
- **Buy in the original sealed packaging.** It shows what the medicine is, and CBP officers can read it.
- **Keep the receipt and any prescription** with the medicine.
- **Buy for personal use only.** The FDA generally looks at quantities of up to a 90-day supply. The details and exceptions are in the [prescription guide](/blog/bringing-prescription-medication-back-from-mexico/).

## Which bridge to use

| Starting from | Crossing to check first | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Downtown El Paso, on foot | [Paso del Norte](/crossing/el-paso-paso-del-norte-pdn/) | It connects downtown El Paso to downtown Juárez, and CBP reports a pedestrian lane there. |
| Central El Paso, driving | [Bridge of the Americas](/crossing/el-paso-bridge-of-the-americas-bota/) | Known locally as Puente Libre. |
| East El Paso, driving | [Ysleta-Zaragoza](/crossing/el-paso-ysleta/) | The eastern bridge, which can save a drive across town. |
| Las Cruces or southern New Mexico | [Santa Teresa](/crossing/santa-teresa-santa-teresa-port-of-entry/) | West of El Paso, in New Mexico. Compare it with the El Paso bridges before you leave. |

Bring a document CBP accepts for re-entry by land, such as a U.S. passport book or passport card. The full list is on [CBP's page](https://www.cbp.gov/travel/us-citizens/western-hemisphere-travel-initiative).
